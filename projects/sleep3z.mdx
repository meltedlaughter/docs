Sleep is the most universal human behaviour and one of the most universally neglected. For PMD Solutions Ltd, the challenge was to bring a clinically credible sleep monitoring device into the consumer market in a way that felt premium, personal, and genuinely motivating — not clinical. Sleep3z is the result: a complete product design system spanning brand identity, physical packaging, digital UX, and the rituals that hold it all together.

<Info>
  **Year:** 2017 · **Client:** PMD Solutions Ltd · **Disciplines:** Product Design / Packaging / UX / Branding
</Info>

## The Problem Space

Who controls sleep in a modern household? The research behind Sleep3z pointed to a clear answer: women — particularly career-driven women who manage both their own performance and the health rhythms of those around them. Understanding this wasn't just a demographic exercise. It shaped every design decision, from the colour palette to the placement of the packaging on the bedside table.

The deeper insight was that people don't respect sleep until they understand it. Before anyone will invest in a device or a subscription, they need to go through a self-discovery phase — moments of realisation that their sleep is affecting their work, their mood, their relationships. The product had to earn its place by first earning belief.

## Research Insights

Five key insights guided the design strategy:

- **Self-discovery first.** People need to witness their own sleep data before they'll commit to change. The product's onboarding experience had to surface insight quickly and without friction.
- **Women as household decision makers.** Wives and mothers frequently drive health-related purchases for the family. The brand needed to speak to them without alienating male users.
- **Performance-oriented women as the primary audience.** Young, career-focused women are motivated to optimise their health — sleep is a lever they're already looking for.
- **Habit needs physical and digital touchpoints.** A device alone won't change behaviour. The ritual of using it — the feel of the packaging, the placement in the bedroom, the app check in the morning — all contribute to forming the habit.
- **Sleep problems are universal.** Barriers to sleep are relatable across demographics, which means community features — forums, tips, shared experiences — carry real value.

## Brand Strategy

<Note>
  The choice of purple as the brand's primary colour was deliberate and research-backed: it is the most neutral luxury colour that appeals equally to men and women, avoiding the gendered coding of blue or pink while still reading as premium.
</Note>

The brand was built around the idea that sleep is an investment in performance and well-being — not a passive activity but an active practice. The visual language leans into depth, calm, and quiet confidence. A moodboard centred on creating a sense of depth informed the colour system, typography, and spatial design of all touchpoints.

## Key Design Elements

### The Sleep Dial

The core interaction of the Sleep3z app is the Sleep Dial — a single, intuitive control that becomes the user's daily ritual. Rather than presenting raw data in a dashboard, the Dial gives users a felt sense of their sleep quality: something they can act on immediately and track over time.

### Packaging as Product

The packaging was designed to do more than protect the device in transit. Once unboxed, it was intended to live on the bedside table — functioning as a soft nightlight that occupies the same real-estate as a phone charger, and in doing so, *replacing* that phone charger as the last thing you interact with before sleep. This deliberate physical displacement is one of the design's most considered moves.

### Instructions for Use (IFU)

The IFU was designed to feel like part of the brand experience — not a regulatory afterthought. Clear, warm, and visually consistent with the app and packaging, it reduces the friction between unboxing and first use.

### Logo & Visual Identity

The logo and brand system were developed through multiple iterations, tested against the core demographic. The identity had to work across the physical product, the packaging, the app store listing, and the marketing website — maintaining coherence and premium feel across every context.

## UX Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sense Making & Information Architecture">
    Mapped the full ecosystem of touchpoints — device, app, packaging, website, community — and defined the information hierarchy that would connect them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Persona Development">
    Built detailed personas based on the research insights, centring on the career-driven woman and secondary profiles including male partners and family purchasers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flows">
    Designed end-to-end user flows for onboarding, nightly use, morning review, and community engagement — ensuring each moment reinforced the habit loop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="App & Website Design">
    Produced full-fidelity designs for the mobile app and app store presence, as well as the marketing website — all aligned to the brand system.
  </Step>
  <Step title="User Testing">
    Conducted online user testing sessions to validate the UX assumptions, gather feedback on the Sleep Dial interaction, and refine the onboarding experience.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Outcome

Sleep3z demonstrates what's possible when brand strategy, industrial design, packaging, and UX are developed as a unified system rather than handed off in sequence. Every touchpoint — from the first time someone sees the box to the hundredth morning they check their sleep score — was designed to reinforce the same message: sleep is worth taking seriously, and you're the kind of person who does.
