Kevin Glynn is an interaction and product designer based in Ireland whose practice resists easy categorisation. Over nearly a decade of professional work, he has designed consumer hardware packaging and brand systems for healthcare startups, conceived speculative artefacts for design exhibitions, built UX flows for fitness and wellness apps, and created play-space services for urban families. What connects these projects is a consistent question: how does design shape the way people behave, and can it do so more honestly?

## Disciplines & Skills

Kevin's work draws on a broad toolkit built across industrial design education and professional practice in interaction and product design.

| Discipline | What it means in Kevin's practice |
|---|---|
| **Industrial Design** | Form, material, and manufacturing constraints as creative input — not afterthoughts |
| **UX / UI Design** | Flows, information architecture, and interface design grounded in user research |
| **Speculative & Critical Design** | Future-scenario artefacts that provoke questions about technology and society |
| **Brand Identity** | Visual systems — mark, type, colour, motion — built around a clear strategic idea |
| **Service Design** | End-to-end experience mapping, from touchpoint to backstage infrastructure |
| **Voice & Conversational UX** | Designing for ears and mouths: dialogue flows, persona, and the particular craft of VUX |
| **Physical–Digital Interaction** | Products and experiences where atoms and bits are inseparable — NFC, Bluetooth, sensors |

## Philosophy

Kevin's design thinking is shaped by thinkers who understood that designed things are never neutral.

> *"The medium is the message"* — Marshall McLuhan

McLuhan's insistence that the form of a tool shapes its users as much as its content runs through Kevin's physical–digital work. **Anthony Dunne**'s speculative practice showed Kevin that design is a mode of inquiry as much as a mode of production. **Bret Victor**'s demand for immediacy and feedback in design tools informs how Kevin thinks about interface legibility. And **Wilson Miner**'s writing on designed environments as shapers of long-term behaviour grounds Kevin's interest in designing for sustainable habit change rather than momentary engagement.

In practice, this means:

- **Transparency first** — systems and products should reveal their logic. Hidden mechanics breed dependency and erode trust.
- **Behaviour change by design** — the best products work with the grain of human psychology, not against it.
- **Physical and digital as one** — the boundary between screen and world is a design problem, not a given constraint.
