Light is one of the most intimate design materials there is. Unlike furniture or architecture, which we encounter through touch and movement, light shapes the way we *perceive* everything else in a room — its warmth, its shadow, its sense of enclosure or openness. Designing lighting products is therefore as much about designing experience as it is about designing objects. This series of concepts, developed in collaboration with Gary Bainton for Mullan Lighting, approaches that challenge with both material seriousness and atmospheric sensitivity.

## The Context: Mullan Lighting

Mullan Lighting is an Irish lighting manufacturer with a strong tradition in handcrafted, design-led product. Their work spans residential and hospitality markets, with a particular strength in pendant and feature lighting that combines historical reference with contemporary production. Working within their design language meant engaging seriously with materials — metal, glass, woven elements — and with the formal vocabulary of lighting as a craft discipline.

## Collaboration with Gary Bainton

The concepts were developed as a creative collaboration between Kevin Glynn and Gary Bainton, bringing together complementary design perspectives. Collaborative product development of this kind introduces productive friction — decisions about form, proportion, material, and manufacturing viability are tested against two different sets of instincts and experiences. The result is typically more resolved than solo work, and these concepts reflect that: each idea was pushed further, questioned more rigorously, and refined through genuine dialogue.

## Design Approach

The brief for these concepts asked for a fresh perspective on Mullan's existing design language — not a departure, but an evolution. The concepts explore how industrial forms can be reworked to produce objects with genuine atmospheric presence: pieces that earn their place in a room by doing more than simply holding a bulb.

Each concept in the series investigates a different formal strategy: some pursue simplicity and reduction, letting material quality do the expressive work; others introduce structural complexity that creates interesting shadow patterns and distributes light in unexpected ways. In all cases, the designs were considered as manufacturing proposals — not purely speculative, but grounded in the real constraints of production.

## Formal and Material Decisions

Material choices throughout the concepts respond to Mullan's established expertise and supplier relationships. Metal remains central — its weight and permanence lending the objects a quality that positions them clearly in the premium segment. Surface treatment is used thoughtfully: the difference between a brushed and a polished finish carries significant meaning in this context, shifting a piece from industrial to domestic, from workshop to sitting room.

Proportion is treated as a primary design variable. Scale affects how light distributes and how a fixture reads in a space. Several of the concepts work with exaggerated proportion — unusually generous shades, unexpectedly slender stems — to create pieces with a distinct physical presence rather than blending into the visual background of a room.

## Outcome

The concepts demonstrate a coherent design sensibility applied to a demanding brief — one that required both formal invention and material pragmatism. For Mullan Lighting, the work represents an exploration of design directions that could extend their range into new formal territory while remaining recognisably within their craft tradition.
