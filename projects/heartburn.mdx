Most people want to be fitter. Far fewer actually train with any structure. HeartBurn was designed for that gap — the space between good intentions and consistent effort — using one of the body's most honest signals, heart rate, as both the metric and the social connector. It's a fitness app concept that reframes competition not as winning or losing, but as a reason to push a little harder than you would have alone.

<Info>
  **Year:** 2019 · **Disciplines:** UX / UI / Micro-interaction Design
</Info>

## The Problem

Exercise without structure produces inconsistent results and makes it nearly impossible for people to understand their own fitness levels over time. Most consumer fitness apps either overwhelm users with data or reduce everything to step counts and calorie approximations. Neither approach builds the kind of intrinsic motivation that sustains a long-term habit.

Heart rate is different. It's real-time, visceral, and universal — it shows exactly how hard your body is working in any given moment, regardless of age, fitness level, or the type of exercise being done. It's also deeply human: two people with the same elevated heart rate are, in a very real sense, sharing an experience — even if they're on opposite sides of the world.

## The Design Concept

HeartBurn uses heart rate as its primary data point and builds a social layer on top of it. The core idea is that when you can feel — or see — that someone else's heart is working just as hard as yours, you're more likely to keep going. Not because you want to beat them, but because you're in it together.

<Note>
  HeartBurn's goal is not winning or losing. It's about giving you a reason to push a little further than you would have without that connection. The competitive element is a vehicle for effort, not an end in itself.
</Note>

## Key Features

### Onboarding Flow

The onboarding experience was designed to set expectations clearly while getting users to their first meaningful interaction as quickly as possible. Rather than a lengthy setup process, onboarding focuses on connecting the user's heart rate data and introducing them to the concept of shared effort — framed warmly, not competitively.

Micro-interactions throughout the onboarding sequence were crafted to feel alive and responsive, using animation and haptic timing to mirror the organic rhythm of the heart rate data the app is built around.

### Global Challenges

The standout feature is Global Challenges — a mechanism that connects users around the world through shared physical effort. Challenges are defined by heart rate zones and duration, not by distance or pace, making them accessible regardless of how fit you currently are or what kind of exercise you prefer.

Seeing your heart rate alongside others in real time — all working hard, all visible to each other — creates a sense of collective effort that solo training simply can't replicate. The interface communicates this connection clearly: other participants are shown not as names or avatars, but as living heartbeat signals.

## Design Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Problem Framing">
    Researched the gap between fitness intentions and structured training behaviour. Identified heart rate as the most universally accessible and emotionally resonant health metric to build around.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Concept Development">
    Developed the social connectivity model — using shared heart rate data as the basis for connection rather than social profiles or performance rankings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Style Guide & Visual Language">
    Created a visual system that communicates energy, warmth, and effort — balancing the intensity of exercise with the connective, human quality of the product's purpose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="UI Design">
    Designed full-fidelity screens for onboarding, the main dashboard, live challenge views, and post-workout summaries. Each screen was designed around the live, pulsing quality of heart rate data.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Micro-interaction Design">
    Defined animation and transition behaviour throughout the app to make the interface feel physiologically alive — responsive in the way that a heartbeat is responsive.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Outcome

HeartBurn demonstrates how a single, well-chosen data point — heart rate — can become the foundation for a social and motivational experience that feels genuinely different from existing fitness apps. By reframing competition as connection and effort as something shared rather than measured, it opens up fitness tracking to people who have previously felt excluded from the genre's winner-takes-all logic.
