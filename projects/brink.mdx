What happens when the sheer volume of our digital relationships starts to feel less like connection and more like noise? Brink was designed in response to exactly that tension — a provocation and a product rolled into one, asking us to reconsider how we organise, prioritise, and limit the social signals we let into our lives. Rather than relying on software nudges or screen-time settings, Brink proposes a set of physical objects that bring tangible consequence to digital behaviour.

<Info>
  **Year:** 2014 · **Disciplines:** Industrial Design / Product Design / Interaction Design
</Info>

## The Challenge

Digital consumption has become reflexive. Social feeds, notifications, and online relationships accumulate without hierarchy — every connection treated with the same urgency, every piece of content demanding the same attention. Brink was conceived as a response to this flattening: a system that forces users to be deliberate about who and what they let occupy their attention.

The design challenge was not simply to make another app or a soft reminder. It was to create something *physical* — objects that carry weight, require action, and represent real relationships in a way that a list of usernames never can.

## The Design Approach

Brink operates as a three-part ecosystem. Each component addresses a different layer of the problem: how relationships are organised, how they are stored, and how media consumption is metered. The system works in concert — hardware, connectivity, and a companion app — to give users genuine agency over their digital social lives.

A provocation video was produced alongside the physical artefacts to illustrate the overconsumption of digital content and set the context for why such a system might be necessary.

## Key Components

### The Board

The centrepiece of the system is the Board — a circular object organised into concentric rings that function as a hierarchy for social connections. Drawing on the metaphor of electron orbits, relationships are placed closer to or further from the centre depending on their importance. Those in the innermost ring receive the most access and attention; those on the outer edges are managed with greater distance.

This spatial metaphor is immediately legible. Users don't need to read documentation to understand that the centre is more intimate than the edge — the geometry does the communicating.

### Token / Silos

The Tokens — referred to as Silos — are layered acrylic objects embedded with LEDs that illuminate each level as relationships or limits are assigned to them. Each Token is a physical vessel for a social relationship or category of connection.

Via NFC and Bluetooth, the relationships and assigned limits are stored directly onto the objects themselves. The Silos become portable, tactile records of who matters and how much access they have — a kind of material social graph.

### The Portal

The Portal addresses media consumption directly. Rather than a passive interface, it acts as a physical brake on the flow of content — slowing down how quickly a user can move through their media feed. Paired with the companion app, users can assign Token limits and set relational rules, so the Portal enforces the intentions they've already made deliberate.

## Making the Work

Brink was built through hands-on physical fabrication — acrylic cutting and layering, LED integration, electronics prototyping, and iterative assembly. The making process was central to the project's ethos: if the argument is that physical objects carry more weight than digital ones, then the work itself had to demonstrate craft and material consideration.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Concept & Provocation">
    Developed the core idea of a social circuit breaker through research into digital overconsumption. Produced a provocation video to frame the problem space and communicate urgency.
  </Step>
  <Step title="System Architecture">
    Defined the three-component system — Board, Token/Silos, and Portal — and how they work together to give users hierarchical control over relationships and consumption.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Physical Fabrication">
    Built the objects through iterative prototyping: layered acrylic construction, LED integration, and NFC/Bluetooth connectivity embedded in the Silo tokens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="App Integration">
    Designed the companion app interface to allow users to assign token limits, define relationships, and configure Portal behaviour — connecting the physical objects to digital rules.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Outcome

Brink stands as both a functional prototype and a design argument. It demonstrates that interaction design can take a position on social behaviour — not through restriction or punishment, but through giving people the tools to enact the priorities they already have. The system's physical nature is inseparable from its purpose: holding a Token that represents a person is a categorically different act than tapping a name on a screen.
